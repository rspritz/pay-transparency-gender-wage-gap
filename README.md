# pay-transparency-gender-wage-gap
In recent years, several states and municipalities within the US have adopted pay transparency during hiring laws requiring companies to list salary ranges when posting job vacancies in order to target gender pay inequality. I exploit the staggered adoption of these policies across US states and municipalities to determine the causal impact of these pay transparency during hiring policies on the gender wage gap using a stacked triple differences approach and micro-data from the US Current Population Survey. I find that these policies compressed the gender wage gap by 7 to 8% in adopting jurisdictions, a result that is robust to several specifications. Women at the top end of the earnings distribution saw the largest gains relative to men, where the gender wage gap has persisted most, leading to a marginal increase in the dispersion of female wages. Gains across occupation categories were not uniform, with some categories experiencing a widening of the gender wage gap. Hence, pay transparency during hiring policies may be most effective where wage setting has a high degree of discretion involved. My findings contribute to understanding the efficacy of policy intervention in correcting inequality within labor markets. As these pay transparency policies are generally easy to implement and low cost, more states in the US and countries are looking to implement. These results highlight where these policies may succeed and where further consideration may be needed.
